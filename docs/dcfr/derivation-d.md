## Overview

*River → turn → flop → preflop, with best-response certificates*

This document derives a solver and an auditable stopping rule for the exact R1 game. The [game specification](../r1-game-specification.md) governs; the [reference kernel](../../hunl_r1/kernel.py) supplies the executable transition, chance, observation, ranking, and payoff definitions; the [preflop tree](../../data/preflop-tree.json) supplies the complete exported preflop adjacency. The deliverable is a derivation, not a claim that an equilibrium strategy has been computed.

For each specified street entry \(e\), with a specified compatible private-hand distribution \(q_e\), the target is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Expl}_e(\bar\sigma;q_e)\le 0.005P_e.}
\tag{0.1}
\]

Here exploitability is **half the sum of the two unilateral improvement opportunities**. At the original preflop entry \(P_e=1.5\) BB, so the target is **0.0075 BB per hand**. The corresponding sum of improvements must be at most **0.015 BB**. The betting engine's preflop variable \(P=2C=0\) is not this entry pot.

The street sections are dependency derivations: the root is the certified inequality; its children are the candidate strategy and its verified best responses; their descendants reach explicit array operations, exact card rules, and terminal arithmetic. The order of derivation is river, turn, flop, preflop. Reused equations are textual expansions of already derived operations, not external solver or evaluator oracles.

**Central distinction.** A backward pass evaluates a fixed profile. An equilibrium solve changes both players' strategies through repeated passes. Solving one continuation at one pair of ranges does not make its equilibrium value a range-independent terminal payoff for the previous street.

The following assumptions are explicit.

1. The game is the finite, two-player, zero-sum, perfect-recall R1 EFG. Both players start with 100 BB including posted blinds. There is no rake, ante, side pot, extra board, or suit tie break. All actions and chance supports are exactly those generated by [R1].
2. A solve entry is a **complete fixed public history**, normally immediately after that street's board reveal. Its prefix remains part of every descendant information-set key. A street entry with hidden hands is a public continuation with an initial private-state distribution, not a perfect-information singleton root.
3. Boundary distributions are fixed during a particular standalone solve. When a parent profile changes a boundary distribution, that is a different solve query or part of a joint full-suffix solve.
4. The mathematical reference uses exact probabilities and real arithmetic. A GPU implementation may use floating point to produce a candidate, but the claimed bound must include verified numerical error or use outward-rounded interval best responses.
5. All legal private hands and full histories are retained. Structurally impossible card combinations may be masked; zero strategy probability does not delete histories or information sets.
6. Sufficient storage and work are assumed for the conceptual tabular algorithm. Vectorization and tiling do not establish that the complete exact-card, full-history game fits on one GPU.

### 1.2 Exploitability, one-sided gaps, and what is certified

Let \(p=0\) be SB and \(p=1\) be BB. Let \(U_p^e(\sigma_0,\sigma_1;q_e)\) be expected **whole-hand net BB utility** from continuation \(e\), using the original terminal accounting. Then \(U_1=-U_0\).

For a fixed opponent strategy define

\[
B_p^e(\sigma_{-p};q_e)
=\max_{\tau_p\in\Sigma_p(e)}
U_p^e(\tau_p,\sigma_{-p};q_e),
\]

where \(\Sigma_p(e)\) consists of behavioral strategies that use one distribution per exact information set. Define

\[
g_p^e(\sigma;q_e)=B_p^e(\sigma_{-p};q_e)-U_p^e(\sigma;q_e)\ge0,
\]

\[
\operatorname{NashConv}_e=g_0^e+g_1^e,\qquad
\operatorname{Expl}_e=\frac{g_0^e+g_1^e}{2}
=\frac{B_0^e+B_1^e}{2}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The last equality uses zero sum and removes cancellation-prone on-policy values from the certificate. Individual best-response payoffs can be negative; their sum is nonnegative. A bound \(\operatorname{Expl}_e\le\epsilon_e\) implies \(g_p^e\le2\epsilon_e\), not necessarily \(g_p^e\le\epsilon_e\).

At an ordinary postflop street entry, \(P_e=2C_e\), fixed throughout that solve. Do not replace it by a later enlarged pot when checking the same root. At preflop, the initial live pot is \(1/2+1=1.5\) BB. Called-all-in suffixes contain no decisions; their continuation evaluation requires no equilibrium solve.

With verified upper bounds \(B_p^e\le B_p^{e,+}\), the actual stopping test is

\[
\boxed{\frac{B_0^{e,+}+B_1^{e,+}}{2}\le0.005P_e.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The threshold is the exact rational \(P_e/200\). Form the BR upper endpoint with outward rounding and compare it to that exact rational or a downward-rounded threshold; an unchecked upward-rounded decimal evaluation of 0.005 is not the acceptance rule.

If the requested convention were the full NashConv instead, its numerical threshold would differ by a factor of two. This document consistently uses (1.1).

### 1.3 Exact public histories, private indices, and information sets

A public node \(n\) identifies the **entire** transcript: fixed blind record; every action's street, actor, kind, and finalized target; and every public reveal with its street. It also identifies the pending-deal, active, runout, fold, or showdown mode. Monetary state is metadata, never the node identity.

The board is \(B_n=(F_1,F_2,F_3,t,r)\), truncated to zero, three, four, or five cards, with only \(F_1<F_2<F_3\) sorted. Turn and river are never globally resorted in a history key.

Use the global physical-hand list

\[
\mathcal H=\{(a,b):0\le a<b<52\},\qquad H=1326.
\]

A concrete zero-based lexicographic index is

\[
\operatorname{id}(a,b)=\frac{a(103-a)}2+(b-a-1).
\tag{1.3}
\]

The full hidden history at a public node is represented by \((n,i,j)\), where \(i\) is SB's hand index and \(j\) is BB's. This is a tensor representation of histories, not a merger. The acting player's information set is exactly

\[
I_p(n,k)=(p,\mathcal H[k],B_n,\operatorname{pub}(n)).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Only the opponent-hand index varies within this information set. There is no card bucketing, suit canonicalization of observations, money-state merging, or sharing of mutable strategy rows between equal-pot histories. The two preflop histories reaching \(C=28\), and lead-call versus check-bet-call histories, remain distinct. Perfect recall follows because the later key preserves the player's own hand and the complete ordered prefix of earlier observations and own actions. [R1, §7; K, Game, play, deal, observation, infoset_key.]

### 1.4 Card and action masks

Represent hand \(k=\{a,b\}\) by the 64-bit mask \(h_k=2^a\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}2^b\), and the board by \(b_n=\mathrm{OR}_{c\in B_n}2^c\). All 52 bits fit in an unsigned 64-bit integer. Define

\[
d_n[k]=\mathbf1[(h_k\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}b_n)=0],
\]

\[
M[i,j]=\mathbf1[(h_i\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}h_j)=0],\qquad
M_n[i,j]=d_n[i]d_n[j]M[i,j].
\tag{1.5}
\]

Let \(A_n\) be the exact deduplicated action count and \(\ell[n,a]\) its padded action mask. The maximum axis length is \(A_{\max}=6\): preflop has at most 4 actions, flop/turn at most 5, river at most 6. An action-row mask is \(m[n,k,a]=d_n[k]\ell[n,a]\). Pair validity never becomes an extra action restriction dependent on the unseen opponent hand.

| Street | Board cards | Valid own hands \(\binom{52-b}{2}\) | Opponent hands per valid own hand \(\binom{50-b}{2}\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Preflop | 0 | 1326 | 1225 |
| Flop | 3 | 1176 | 1081 |
| Turn | 4 | 1128 | 1035 |
| River | 5 | 1081 | 990 |

Invalid padded hand rows are zero and excluded from reductions. Valid hands with zero boundary probability still have legal strategy rows.

### 1.5 Compatible boundary ranges

For a fixed complete public entry \(e\), supply nonnegative hand-likelihood factors \(\beta_0,\beta_1\in\mathbb R_+^H\), zero on board-incompatible hands. Define

\[
Z_e=\sum_{i,j}M_e[i,j]\beta_0[i]\beta_1[j],\qquad
q_e[i,j]=\frac{M_e[i,j]\beta_0[i]\beta_1[j]}{Z_e},
\quad Z_e>0.
\tag{1.6}
\]

These factors are often called ranges, but they are **not generally the normalized marginals**. The actual marginal is

\[
q_{e,0}[i]=\frac{\beta_0[i]\sum_jM_e[i,j]\beta_1[j]}{Z_e},
\tag{1.7}
\]

and similarly for BB. Multiplying two already blocker-conditioned marginals generally does not reconstruct the joint. Scaling either factor by a positive constant leaves \(q_e\) unchanged.

Why (1.6) applies to a reachable R1 public history: the root deal probability and each past board denominator are constant across compatible private assignments. Every past action probability of player \(p\), at that fixed transcript, is a function of its own hand. Thus the joint posterior is the blocker mask times the two players' past action-likelihood products, normalized once.

An arbitrary correlated boundary distribution can instead be supplied as a full nonnegative \(H\times H\) tensor \(q_e\), supported on \(M_e\), with total mass one. The general tensor formulas below remain valid. A joint distribution cannot in general be recovered from arbitrary given marginals alone. A nonfactorable distribution is not asserted to be induced by an ordinary R1 public history.

If \(Z_e=0\), the on-policy conditional distribution and conditional exploitability are undefined. Do not manufacture a zero certificate. This does not authorize deleting the corresponding global histories: a unilateral deviation can change its own action-likelihood factor and make the entry reachable.

### 1.6 Tensor contract

Let \(N\) be the number of public nodes in the specified suffix and \(N_D\) its player-decision nodes. Node arrays are ragged by street, board, and action count; the following dense shapes define their semantics.

| Quantity | Shape | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hand cards / hand masks | \([H,2]\), \([H]\) | Physical private pairs / uint64 masks |
| board cards / masks | \([N,5]\), \([N]\) | Street-labelled board, padded slots / uint64 |
| actor, mode, parent, child offsets | \([N]\) | Immutable full-history topology metadata |
| targets, action kinds, legal masks | \([N_D,6]\) | Exact finalized actions; illegal padding masked |
| \(d_n\), \(M_n\) | \([N,H]\), logical \([N,H,H]\) | Hand and pair masks; pair masks generated in tiles |
| \(\sigma,R,S\) | \([N_D,H,6]\) each | Acting player's strategy, signed regret, average numerator |
| \(\rho\) | \([N,2,H]\) | Each player's own action realization reach |
| \(F,J\) | \([N,2,H]\) | Counterfactual policy values / BR values |
| \(Q,r\) | \([N_D,H,6]\) | Acting player's CF action values / instantaneous regrets |
| \(\beta\), \(q_e\) | \([2,H]\), logical \([H,H]\) | Boundary factors / normalized joint |
| \(\kappa_n\) | \([N]\) | Product of board-edge probabilities, i.e. reciprocal denominators, since entry |
| rank code | \([\text{board batch},H]\) | Exact best-five integer for each compatible hand |
| optional \(V_p\) | \([\text{active nodes},H,H]\) | Pairwise fixed-profile continuation values |

“Logical” does not require materializing the full tensor. Pair contractions can be tiled; node values can be streamed by a reverse-topological frontier; immutable transition or rank computations can be reused. Mutable \(R,S,\sigma\), and their exact history identities, remain separate.

## 2. Common exact monetary and history compiler

These are structural leaves of every street hierarchy. Their arithmetic is performed before floating-point learning.

### 2.1 State, passive actions, and closure

At a player node use the source state

\[
(s,p,P,C,(c_0,c_1),i,\Delta),\quad P=2C,\quad
a=c_p,\ f=c_{1-p},\ R_{\max}=100-C,\ Q_{\rm call}=P+2f.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The live pot is \(P+c_0+c_1\). Acting to target \(b\) contributes \(b-a\); the player's uncommitted stack is \(R_{\max}-a\). \(Q_{\rm call}\), not the unmatched live pot, sizes a pot-fraction raise.

When \(a<f\), passive actions are fold and call-to-\(f\). When a check is available, there is no fold or call. The initial SB call-to-1 is the sole nonclosing call: it leaves the BB option. BB's option check closes preflop at matched target 1. Postflop BB's opening check passes to SB; SB's following check closes at matched target 0. An ordinary call closes immediately.

Closing at target \(x\) sets \(C'=C+x\), exactly once. River closure produces showdown. Before river, \(C'=100\) produces a chance-only runout; otherwise it produces the next board chance node and only then an unopened BB decision with \(P'=2C'\), current contributions zero, and \(i=\Delta=0\).

A finalized aggression sets \(c_p'=b\), switches actor, increments \(i\), and sets \(\Delta'=b-f\), the **actual finalized increment**, including a short all-in. A jam is not terminal: its response is fold/call. [R1, §§3, 6; K, step, _settle, advance_board.]

If target ledgers are retained, preflop starts with \(B_{0,0}=1/2,B_{0,1}=1\), and each postflop street starts with \(B_{s,0}=0\). Only finalized aggressive actions append targets. Passive actions do not append targets; a completed street's matched target enters \(C\) exactly once. Every betting street skipped after a called all-in has \(k_s=0,B_{s,0}=0\), with matched total commitment remaining 100. These ledger zeros create no artificial public actions.

### 2.2 Exact ordered resolver and redundant caps

Before generating aggression require \(f<R_{\max}\) and \(i<K_s\), with \(K_0=100\) and \(K_s=R_{\max}\) postflop. For each offered raw rational target \(x=n/d\), independently compute

\[
b_0=\left\lfloor\frac{2n+d}{2d}\right\rfloor,\qquad
m_{\min}=
\begin{cases}
1&\text{unbet postflop opening},\\
f+\Delta&\text{raise, including BB's preflop option},
\end{cases}
\]

\[
b_1=\max(b_0,m_{\min}),
\]

\[
b=
\begin{cases}
100-C,&C+b_1\ge100,\ \text{or }(s=0\land b_1>40),\\
&\qquad\text{or }(s\in\{1,2\}\land3(C+b_1)>200),\\
b_1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Finally sort and deduplicate the finalized targets. Action identity is kind plus finalized target. Raw aliases get neither extra histories nor extra strategy probability.

This is **eligibility → positive half-up → minimum → mandatory snap → deduplication**. Use integer rational arithmetic; no binary float rounding or ties-to-even. Do not reject a minimum above \(R_{\max}\) before snapping it back to \(R_{\max}\). A non-all-in must satisfy the ordinary minimum; a final all-in may be short. Preflop 40 is unsnapped but 41 snaps; on flop/turn with \(C=30\), 36 remains ordinary while 37 snaps to 70. These examples do not create new raw candidates.

The cap is redundant, but its specified test remains part of the compiler. Initially \(i\le f\); after any aggression to integer \(b>f\), \(i+1\le f+1\le b\). Therefore at a reachable facing node \(i\le f<R_{\max}\). An unopened postflop node has \(i=0<R_{\max}\); preflop similarly has \(i<100\) whenever a raise is stack-eligible. No smaller raise-depth or aggression cap may be introduced. [R1, §§3.3, 4.1.]

### 2.3 Compilation without state merging

Build a fresh node identity whenever an edge appends a public action or reveal. Generate all legal successors, including distinct copies of identical monetary continuation templates. A pending deal has chance children, not exposed future player actions. A fold has no children. A runout has only its required chance children.

Structural arrays may be cached by monetary state to generate menus. That cache does not own a strategy row. A public node plus a physical own hand owns its row under (1.4). Canonical sorting of an unordered pair or flop is serialization; sorting turn/river labels, canonicalizing suits in observations, or identifying equal monetary states would change the EFG.

The tree is finite because every aggressive target increases an integer bounded by \(R_{\max}\); passive transitions close, terminate, or make the one allowed nonclosing limp/first-check transition; and street/chance progress is finite. This justifies exact forward and reverse topological schedules. [R1, §§6.4, 7.]

### R. Root: a BR-certified river continuation

### R.1 → R.1.1: the candidate is an average realization plan

Let \(\rho_p^t(n,k)\) be player \(p\)'s own action reach from \(e\) to \((n,k)\), excluding chance and opponent actions. Its construction appears below. At a row owned by \(p\), the candidate is

\[
\bar\sigma^T[n,k,a]=
\frac{\sum_{t=1}^T t^\gamma\rho_p^t(n,k)\sigma^t[n,k,a]}
{\sum_{t=1}^T t^\gamma\rho_p^t(n,k)},
\quad \gamma=2,
\tag{R.1}
\]

when the denominator is positive. If it is zero, choose the uniform distribution on the legal actions. Board-incompatible rows remain all zero and are not strategies of a legal information set.

An online, explicitly indexed accumulator gives exactly this result:

\[
S^0=0,\qquad
S^t[n,k,a]=
\left(\frac{t}{t+1}\right)^\gamma
\left(S^{t-1}[n,k,a]
+\rho_p^t(n,k)\sigma^t[n,k,a]\right).
\tag{R.2}
\]

Normalize a valid row by \(D^T[n,k]=\sum_aS^T[n,k,a]\). Both the added term and the old sum receive the discount. A contribution made in round \(j\) is multiplied by

\[
\prod_{t=j}^T\left(\frac{t}{t+1}\right)^\gamma
=\left(\frac{j}{T+1}\right)^\gamma.
\]

The common factor \((T+1)^{-\gamma}\) cancels, proving (R.1). Discounting only the old sum with the same indexing would give a different, shifted sequence of weights.

Why own reach appears: for the player's sequence preceding information set \(I\), let \(x_p^t(s)\) be its realization weight. Perfect recall makes this one number, independent of the hidden opponent hand. The weighted average sequence plan is \(\bar x_p(s)=\sum_t t^\gamma x_p^t(s)/\sum_t t^\gamma\). Its behavioral probability is

\[
\frac{\bar x_p(sa)}{\bar x_p(s)}
=\frac{\sum_t t^\gamma x_p^t(s)\sigma_p^t(I,a)}
{\sum_t t^\gamma x_p^t(s)}.
\]

This is (R.1). Chance reach, opponent reach, and joint visitation frequency do not belong in this averaging weight. The average of both players' behavior at a descendant is also not reconstructed by independently averaging hand-pair visitation.

### R.1.1.1: regret matching, discounts, initialization, and ordering

Use the DCFR parameters \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(3/2,0,2)\). The discount family and these conventional parameters are from [DCFR]; the following storage convention makes every iteration boundary explicit.

Initialize signed regrets \(R^0[n,k,a]=0\) and average accumulators \(S^0[n,k,a]=0\). On every valid row initialize the first policy uniformly over the **deduplicated** legal actions.

At round \(t\ge1\), freeze the entire profile using

\[
x[n,k,a]=\ell[n,a]\max(R^{t-1}[n,k,a],0),\qquad
z[n,k]=\sum_a x[n,k,a],
\]

\[
\sigma^t[n,k,a]=
\begin{cases}
x[n,k,a]/z[n,k],&z[n,k]>0,\\
\ell[n,a]/A_n,&z[n,k]=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{R.3}
\]

Invalid hand rows and padded actions are zero. This requires elementwise positive part, masked reduction, division, and a masked uniform fallback; it is not a softmax. A branchless GPU division may use denominator \(z\) where \(z>0\) and the harmless placeholder 1 elsewhere, then select the uniform fallback. Do not evaluate an unguarded \(0/0\), add an epsilon, or clamp a small positive denominator to 1.

After computing instantaneous regrets \(r^t\) from this same frozen profile, form

\[
X^t=R^{t-1}+r^t,\qquad
d_t^+=\frac{t^{3/2}}{t^{3/2}+1},\quad
d_t^-=\frac12,
\]

\[
R^t[n,k,a]=
\begin{cases}
d_t^+X^t[n,k,a],&X^t[n,k,a]>0,\\
d_t^-X^t[n,k,a],&X^t[n,k,a]<0,\\
0,&X^t[n,k,a]=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{R.4}
\]

The sign is tested **after adding the new regret**. Negative regrets are retained and halved, not clipped to zero. \(t^{3/2}=t\sqrt t\) is a single round scalar. Padded entries stay zero. Then apply (R.2) using the already frozen \(\sigma^t,\rho^t\).

This is a post-add, post-round discount convention. If an implementation instead stores undiscounted post-add totals, the next round's pre-add discount must be indexed by the previous round. Mixing those conventions silently changes the algorithm.

All player updates in this document are simultaneous: compute both players' values and regrets under one frozen \(\sigma^t\), then commit both regret tables. Updating SB and immediately evaluating BB against the changed SB strategy is a different alternating schedule and is not the schedule proved here.

### R.1.1.1.1: counterfactual action advantages

At node \(n\) owned by \(p\), let \(Q_p^t[n,k,a]\) be the unnormalized counterfactual value of choosing \(a\) now and following \(\sigma_p^t\) later, against \(\sigma_{-p}^t\). Define

\[
F_p^t[n,k]=\sum_{a\in A_n}\sigma^t[n,k,a]Q_p^t[n,k,a],
\]

\[
r^t[n,k,a]=Q_p^t[n,k,a]-F_p^t[n,k].
\tag{R.5}
\]

The counterfactual weighting includes chance and the opponent's prefix play. It excludes **all** of \(p\)'s prefix action reach. Consequently no \(\rho_p^t(n,k)\) multiplies (R.5). Using such a factor would prevent learning about an action sequence that the player currently never reaches.

The next dependency is an exact way to compute \(Q\) without retaining a private-pair value matrix at every node.

### R.1.1.1.1.1: forward reaches, then vector backward values

For each valid entry hand set \(\rho_p^t(e,k)=1\). At a player edge \(n\xrightarrow{a}n'\) owned by seat \(p_n\),

\[
\rho_{p_n}^t(n',k)=\rho_{p_n}^t(n,k)\sigma_{p_n}^t[n,k,a],\qquad
\rho_{1-p_n}^t(n',k)=\rho_{1-p_n}^t(n,k).
\tag{R.6}
\]

On a board edge, copy both own action reaches to its compatible child hands. Mask incompatible hands to zero. That mask is a support restriction, not a probabilistic chance factor.

Let \(\kappa_n\) be the product of the exact board-edge probabilities since \(e\) for a compatible private pair. On river \(\kappa_n=1\). In the later street expansions it is multiplied by the appropriate reciprocal denominator on every board edge. Define the chance-prefix pair measure

\[
\lambda_n[i,j]=q_e[i,j]M_n[i,j]\kappa_n.
\tag{R.7}
\]

At a terminal \(z\), contract over the hidden opponent hand:

\[
F_0^t[z,i]
=\sum_j\lambda_z[i,j]\rho_1^t(z,j)u_0(z;i,j),
\]

\[
F_1^t[z,j]
=\sum_i\lambda_z[i,j]\rho_0^t(z,i)u_1(z;i,j).
\tag{R.8}
\]

Each output has shape \([H]\). The BB contraction sums the SB axis; it is not obtained by merely negating SB's already-contracted vector, because the weights differ.

Then visit nodes in reverse topological order. For traverser \(p\):

\[
F_p^t[n,k]=
\begin{cases}
\sum_a\sigma_p^t[n,k,a]F_p^t[n_a,k],
&\operatorname{actor}(n)=p,\\
\sum_aF_p^t[n_a,k],
&\operatorname{actor}(n)=1-p,\\
\sum_cF_p^t[n_c,k],
&n\text{ is chance}.
\end{cases}
\tag{R.9}
\]

At a node owned by \(p\), set \(Q_p^t[n,k,a]=F_p^t[n_a,k]\). The river instantiation uses only the first two cases. Turn/flop/preflop use the chance case as well.

**Do not multiply opponent probabilities or chance probabilities again in the last two cases.** They are already present in the terminal weights through \(\rho_{-p}\) and \(\kappa\). Own probabilities are absent from those weights and appear only in the first case.

To prove (R.9), expand the definition

\[
F_p^\sigma[n,k]=
\sum_l\lambda_n^{(p)}[k,l]\rho_{-p}(n,l)
V_p^\sigma(n;k,l),
\tag{R.10}
\]

where \(V_p^\sigma\) is the conditional continuation payoff for both fixed hands, and \(\lambda_n^{(0)}[k,l]=\lambda_n[k,l]\), \(\lambda_n^{(1)}[k,l]=\lambda_n[l,k]\). At an own action, its probability depends on \(k\) and can be pulled outside the sum. At an opponent action its probability becomes part of the child's \(\rho_{-p}\). At chance its probability and compatibility become part of the child's \(\lambda\). This gives all three cases by induction from (R.8).

At the entry, \(U_p^e(\sigma;q_e)=\sum_kF_p^\sigma[e,k]\). At a descendant node, its on-policy contribution is \(\sum_k\rho_p(n,k)F_p[n,k]\). These \(F\) vectors are not normalized conditional hand EVs; no division by a vanishing reach is required.

### R.1.1.1.1.1.1: exact river topology and terminal contractions

At an ordinary river entry, BB acts first with zero current contributions. The raw opening targets for either a BB lead or an SB bet after a check are

\[
\{P/2,P,3P/2,R_{\max}\}
=\{C,2C,3C,100-C\}.
\tag{R.11}
\]

At every eligible response, including a check-raise and every subsequent re-raise, the raw targets are

\[
\{f+\Delta,\ f+Q_{\rm call}/2,\ f+Q_{\rm call},\ R_{\max}\}
=\{f+\Delta,\ C+2f,\ 2C+3f,\ 100-C\}.
\tag{R.12}
\]

Apply the exact resolver (2.2), with only the stack snap on river. Thus an explicit all-in and a snapped ordinary target can become one action. Retain the full-minimum candidate \(f+\Delta\).

The recursive productions are:

- Unopened BB: check to an SB node, or each finalized bet to a facing SB node.
- SB after BB checks: check to showdown at \(C\), or each finalized bet to a facing BB node.
- Facing actor \(p\): fold; call to showdown at \(C+f\); or every finalized raise to a facing \(1-p\) node with old faced contribution \(f\), new target \(b\), level \(i+1\), and increment \(b-f\).

If \(f=R_{\max}\), the response is exactly fold/call. There is no extra prior-aggressor action after a call and no third check. Each production appends a new full transcript. [R1, §§4.2, 6.]

For a terminal pair tile \((i,j)\), generate \(M_z[i,j]\) by uint64 AND and equality-to-zero. Multiply this Boolean mask, \(q_e[i,j]\), \(\kappa_z\), the opponent reach, and the exact net payoff. Reduce along \(j\) for SB or \(i\) for BB. One thread block can own one tile of own hands; partial sums over opponent tiles require a defined reduction order or interval enclosure.

### R.1.1.1.1.1.1.1: best-five ranking and net payoff, fully expanded

Card \(c\in\{0,\ldots,51\}\) has rank \(2+\lfloor c/4\rfloor\) and suit \(c\bmod4\). For a valid hand and five-card board, concatenate seven cards and gather the fixed list of \(\binom75=21\) five-element index subsets. The scratch tensor is \([\text{board batch},H,21,5]\), with invalid hand rows masked.

For each five-card subset:

1. Form rank counts \(n_r=\sum_{j=1}^5\mathbf1[r_j=r]\) for \(r=2,\ldots,14\), a length-13 integer histogram. Form suit counts \(s_v\) for \(v=0,\ldots,3\). A flush means \(\max_vs_v=5\).
2. Sort the five ranks descending. A fixed odd-even compare-exchange network with five alternating phases suffices: compare positions \((0,1),(2,3)\) on even phases and \((1,2),(3,4)\) on odd phases, placing the larger rank first.
3. For straight highs \(h=6,\ldots,14\), test that every rank in \(\{h-4,\ldots,h\}\) is present. For \(h=5\), test \(\{14,2,3,4,5\}\). Select the largest passing high, or zero. With five cards this also requires five distinct ranks and permits no other wraparound.
4. Obtain quad, trip, pair, and singleton ranks by masked maxima over the length-13 histogram. To extract several descending ranks, repeatedly take the masked maximum and clear that rank. This specifies the required top-k operations without a hand-strength oracle.
5. Select the highest applicable category below and concatenate its tie-break fields; pad the tuple to six integers with zeros.

| Category | First field | Remaining fields, in priority order |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| High card | 0 | Five descending ranks |
| One pair | 1 | Pair rank, three descending singleton kickers |
| Two pair | 2 | Higher pair, lower pair, singleton |
| Trips | 3 | Trip rank, two descending singleton kickers |
| Straight | 4 | Straight high, with wheel high 5 |
| Flush | 5 | Five descending ranks |
| Full house | 6 | Trip rank, pair rank |
| Quads | 7 | Quad rank, singleton |
| Straight flush | 8 | Straight high |

Encode a padded tuple \(v_0,\ldots,v_5\) as

\[
\operatorname{code}_5=\sum_{d=0}^5v_d15^{5-d}.
\tag{R.13}
\]

Every field lies in \(\{0,\ldots,14\}\), so integer comparison is exactly lexicographic comparison. The code is below \(15^6=11{,}390{,}625\) and fits in int32. Set

\[
\operatorname{code}_7(k,B)=
\max_{\text{21 subsets}}\operatorname{code}_5.
\tag{R.14}
\]

This permits zero, one, or both hole cards. Suits never resolve ties. Five- and six-card evaluator checks use the same construction with 1 and 6 subsets respectively. Ranking data can be reused between histories because it is immutable card arithmetic; the associated strategies remain distinct. [R1, §8.1; K, _rank_five_unchecked, rank_five, evaluate.]

If player \(p\) folds, let \(L=C+c_p\) using that player's already contributed amount. Set

\[
u_p=-L,\qquad u_{1-p}=L.
\tag{R.15}
\]

The unmatched opposing wager is not profit. At showdown with final matched commitment \(C_*\), set

\[
u_0(z;i,j)=C_*
\left(\mathbf1[\operatorname{code}_7(i,B)>
\operatorname{code}_7(j,B)]
-\mathbf1[\operatorname{code}_7(i,B)<
\operatorname{code}_7(j,B)]\right),
\quad u_1=-u_0.
\tag{R.16}
\]

The commitment \(C_*\) already includes the final call once. Do not subtract earlier investment a second time. Ties pay zero. All-in showdowns use \(C_*=100\). Every utility is in \([-100,100]\) BB, and final stacks \(100+u_0,100+u_1\) sum to 200. Half-BB integer units represent all terminal utilities exactly. [R1, §§8.2–8.3; K, payoff.]

### R.1.1.1.1.1.1.2: optional exact terminal acceleration

This is an algebraic implementation of the preceding leaf, not a different game. For factored \(q_e\), hold own hand \(k=\{a,b\}\) fixed and let \(w_l=d_n[l]\beta_{-p}[l]\rho_{-p}(n,l)\). Define total weight \(T=\sum_lw_l\) and per-card totals \(T_c=\sum_{l:c\in\mathcal H[l]}w_l\). Then

\[
\sum_lM_n[k,l]w_l=d_n[k](T-T_a-T_b+w_k).
\tag{R.17}
\]

The last term adds back the sole pair containing both cards, which was subtracted twice. Multiplying by the fold utility gives its opponent-weighted terminal vector. The remaining own-prior factor is \(\kappa_n\beta_p[k]/Z_e\).

For showdown, group the opponent weights by exact integer rank code. For a hero code \(v\), prefix sums give

\[
G(v)=\sum_{\operatorname{code}(l)<v}w_l
-\sum_{\operatorname{code}(l)>v}w_l.
\]

Build the same prefix sums for each of the 52 cards, obtaining \(G_c(v)\). The compatible win-minus-loss weight is

\[
d_n[k]\{G(v)-G_a(v)-G_b(v)+w_k\,\operatorname{sgn}(v-\operatorname{code}(k))\}.
\tag{R.18}
\]

The added term is zero here because the identical hand has the identical rank, but it makes the inclusion-exclusion identity explicit. Multiply by \(C_*\) and the own-prior factor. Required primitives are weighted scatter-add into rank bins, inclusive scans, gather at the hero's lower/upper rank boundary, and subtraction. Tied ranks contribute zero. This removes many explicit pair comparisons while retaining every physical hand and every strategy row. For a nonfactorable \(q_e\), use the general tiled contraction or a separately proved decomposition.

### R.2 → R.2.1 → R.2.1.1: infoset-consistent best response

Freeze the **exported average opponent strategy** \(\bar\sigma_{-p}\). Recompute its own action reaches from entry; use neither the last-iterate opponent nor averages of joint visitation or conditional posteriors. The exactly equivalent common-weight average of own realization reaches is discussed after (T.11). Build terminal vectors by (R.8), using the exported opponent's reach. Call them \(J_p[z,k]\).

Reverse the tree with

\[
J_p[n,k]=
\begin{cases}
\max_{a\in A_n}J_p[n_a,k],&\operatorname{actor}(n)=p,\\
\sum_aJ_p[n_a,k],&\operatorname{actor}(n)=1-p,\\
\sum_cJ_p[n_c,k],&n\text{ is chance}.
\end{cases}
\tag{R.19}
\]

Then

\[
B_p^e(\bar\sigma_{-p};q_e)=\sum_kJ_p[e,k].
\tag{R.20}
\]

The maximization is over one row \((n,k)\) **after** all opponent hands have been integrated. A deterministic maximizing action, with ties broken by the first legal action index, defines one legal BR action for the whole information set. The operation
\(\sum_lw_l\max_aV(n_a;k,l)\) is generally larger and illegal: it lets the responder see the opponent's cards.

For the padded GPU maximum, replace illegal action lanes by \(-\infty\) before reducing; do not let their stored zero values compete with negative legal values. Afterward set physically invalid hand rows to zero. The signed legal BR values themselves are never clipped at zero.

**Proof.** At a terminal (R.8) equals its chance/opponent-weighted utility. At an opponent or chance node their action probabilities are already in descendant weights, so expectations add. At a responder node the same action must serve every compatible hidden state; those states have already been summed. Each action's future choices are optimized at their own later full-history information sets. Perfect recall and the full transcript ensure that choosing a different current action does not couple otherwise distinct descendants. Therefore induction gives the optimal legal conditional choice at every responder information set and the exact BR payoff at entry. A responder's zero blueprint reach does not appear anywhere in this proof.

### R.2.2: a numerical certificate, not a training diagnostic

Run (R.8), (R.19), and (R.20) with exact arithmetic or verified intervals. If each child value lies in \([L_a,U_a]\), an own-node BR value lies in

\[
[\max_aL_a,\ \max_aU_a].
\tag{R.21}
\]

No uncertain floating-point argmax has to be trusted. Opponent/chance sums use outward-rounded endpoint sums. Terminal weights and normalized input distributions receive corresponding enclosures. Section 7 specifies every interval operation and proves the resulting error bounds.

Only declare the river target met when (R.0) passes with upper endpoints. A small change in policy, small discounted regret, a sampled response, or a stable on-policy value is not this certificate. DCFR is the candidate-generation mechanism; BR is the independent acceptance mechanism.

### T.1.1.1.1: turn transitions

The raw turn opening menu for both seats is

\[
\{P/2,P\}=\{C,2C\}.
\tag{T.1}
\]

At every eligible response it is

\[
\{f+\Delta,\ C+2f,\ 2C+3f\}.
\tag{T.2}
\]

Retain the same passive productions as river, but a non-all-in closure goes to a pending river deal rather than directly to showdown.

### T.1.1.1.2 → T.1.1.1.2.1: exact river chance

The candidate public-card axis at a fixed four-card board has 48 elements \(c\in D\setminus B\). For each compatible private pair, precisely 44 are legal. Define

\[
K_c(i,j)=
\frac{\mathbf1[c\notin\mathcal H[i]\cup\mathcal H[j]\cup B]}{44}.
\tag{T.3}
\]

For every compatible pair, \(\sum_cK_c(i,j)=1\). The denominator is 44, not the 48 public candidates and not a single player's 46 unseen cards.

For a **fixed continuation profile** \(\tau\), let \(V_p^\tau(n;i,j)\) denote pairwise conditional expected terminal utility. An ordinary closed-turn node \(b\) evaluates as

\[
V_p^\tau(b;i,j)=\sum_{c\in D\setminus B}K_c(i,j)
V_p^\tau(e_{b,c};i,j),
\tag{T.4}
\]

where \(e_{b,c}\) contains the complete turn-close transcript and the labelled river reveal. At each river player node, \(V\) uses an action-probability weighted sum, with SB probabilities indexed by \(i\) and BB probabilities indexed by \(j\). At each terminal it uses (R.15–R.16).

In the compressed CF implementation, instead set \(\kappa_{e_{b,c}}=\kappa_b/44\), apply the child board mask, and sum child \(F\) vectors without another \(1/44\). This is exactly (T.4) under the measure convention of (R.7–R.9).

### T.1.1.1.2.2: ordinary river continuation expansion

For each \(e_{b,c}\) in (T.4), insert a distinct copy of the river topology (R.11–R.12), keeping the full turn prefix and river label. Its fixed-profile value uses (R.15–R.16) at terminals and the conditional action-weighted form of (R.10) internally. Its CF return is computed by (R.8–R.9), with the parent chance factor already included. Its rows are updated by (R.1–R.5) in the same joint suffix iteration. This recursively expands the ordinary continuation all the way to card histograms, rank comparisons, payoff products, and reductions.

### T.1.1.1.2.3: called-all-in values are pure chance contractions

After a called turn all-in, no policy is consulted. The SB pair payoff is

\[
A_0^{\rm turn}(B;i,j)
=\frac{100}{44}
\sum_{\substack{c\in D\setminus B\\
c\notin\mathcal H[i]\cup\mathcal H[j]}}
\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(
\operatorname{code}_7(i,Bc)-\operatorname{code}_7(j,Bc)\right).
\tag{T.5}
\]

Set \(A_1=-A_0\). This can be streamed over river cards and hand-pair tiles using the ranking primitive already derived. It is terminal integration of a chance-only suffix, not a river subgame solve.

### T.1.1.1.2.4: the range-dependent continuation interface

The following construction applies to every later street boundary and is needed before moving backward to flop. Equations (T.6–T.11) assume the factored entry distribution (1.6); the general correlated-prior form is given immediately afterward.

At descendant public boundary \(b\), relative to solve entry \(e\), define unnormalized factors

\[
r_p^b[k]=\beta_p[k]\rho_p(e\to b,k),\qquad
Z_b=\sum_{i,j}M_b[i,j]r_0^b[i]r_1^b[j].
\tag{T.6}
\]

The current joint reach and, when \(Z_b>0\), the conditional private distribution are

\[
\Pr_\sigma^e(b)=\frac{\kappa_b Z_b}{Z_e},\qquad
q_b[i,j]=\frac{M_b[i,j]r_0^b[i]r_1^b[j]}{Z_b}.
\tag{T.7}
\]

This distinguishes absolute reach from a normalized range query. For a next public event \(c\) with pairwise denominator \(d\), before any intervening action,

\[
\Pr(c\mid b,\sigma)=\frac{Z_{b,c}}{dZ_b}.
\tag{T.8}
\]

The public posterior is generally not uniform; the dealer's kernel conditional on both hands remains exactly uniform. Normalization follows because each compatible pair has \(d\) legal events, so \(\sum_cZ_{b,c}=dZ_b\).

For a fixed profile \(\tau_b\) below \(b\), define **own-range-excluding** continuation vectors

\[
\mathcal C_0^{\tau_b}(b,i;r_0,r_1)
=\sum_jM_b[i,j]r_1[j]V_0^{\tau_b}(b;i,j),
\]

\[
\mathcal C_1^{\tau_b}(b,j;r_0,r_1)
=\sum_iM_b[i,j]r_0[i]V_1^{\tau_b}(b;i,j).
\tag{T.9}
\]

They have shape \([2,H]\). Pairwise \(V^{\tau_b}\) is independent of incoming ranges **when the whole continuation policy is fixed**. The contraction is linear in the opponent factor.

If instead \(\tau_b=\operatorname{Solve}(b,r_0,r_1)\), then both the policy and its \(V\) depend on **both ranges**. Thus

\[
(r_0,r_1)\longmapsto
\left(\tau_b(r_0,r_1),
\mathcal C^{\tau_b(r_0,r_1)}(b;r_0,r_1)\right)
\tag{T.10}
\]

is a range-dependent optimization interface, not a fixed payoff table. Here \(\operatorname{Solve}\) means the explicitly derived tabular DCFR loop and BR stopping test on that whole suffix; it introduces no new oracle. Its key includes the exact full prefix, labelled board, monetary state, input distribution, and certificate tolerance. A scalar equilibrium value discards the hand-dependent information required for upstream regrets and safe responses.

For a normalized local solve at \(b\), its root CF value is

\[
F_p^{\rm local}[b,k]=\frac{r_p^b[k]}{Z_b}\mathcal C_p(b,k).
\]

The correct CF vector in the **parent** solve is instead

\[
\boxed{
F_p^{\rm parent}[b,k]
=\frac{\kappa_b\beta_p[k]}{Z_e}\mathcal C_p(b,k).
}
\tag{T.11}
\]

The player's intervening own reach is excluded. Dividing a local CF value by \(r_p^b[k]\) fails when that factor is zero; computing (T.9) directly does not. If \(\beta_p[k]>0\) but \(\rho_p(e\to b,k)=0\), the parent CF value can still be nonzero and is needed to assess a deviation.

If a conditional per-hand EV is desired, divide \(\mathcal C_p(b,k)\) by \(\sum_lM_b^{(p)}[k,l]r_{-p}[l]\) only when that mass is positive. Neither CFR nor the BR certificate requires this potentially ill-conditioned division.

For a general correlated entry prior, retain

\[
W_b[i,j]=M_b[i,j]q_e[i,j]\rho_0(b,i)\rho_1(b,j),\qquad
Z'_b=\sum_{i,j}W_b[i,j].
\]

Then \(\Pr(b)=\kappa_bZ'_b\), and \(q_b=W_b/Z'_b\) when \(Z'_b>0\). The parent CF return is directly

\[
F_p[b,k]=\kappa_b\sum_l
M_b^{(p)}[k,l]q_e^{(p)}[k,l]\rho_{-p}(b,l)V_p(b;k,l).
\tag{T.12}
\]

This is (R.10) without invented range factors or division by own reach. The next-event public posterior is \(Z'_{b,c}/(dZ'_b)\).

For the final average profile, recompute reaches from its exported rows. In exact arithmetic with the same common iteration weights \(w_t=t^2\), realization averaging implies
\(\rho_p^{\bar\sigma}(b,k)=\sum_tw_t\rho_p^t(b,k)/\sum_tw_t\); fixed \(\beta_p\) gives the same identity for \(r_p^b\). What does not commute with averaging is multiplying the two factors and normalizing the blocker-conditioned joint: the posterior induced by \(\bar\sigma\) is generally not the average of the iteration posteriors. Direct recomputation also accounts for the actual numerical normalization of the exported policy.

### T.2: full-suffix BR and turn stopping rule

Apply (R.19) to all turn and river rows against the fixed average opponent. At a river boundary use the responder's river BR continuation against that opponent, not a scalar river equilibrium value and not the candidate's on-policy river value.

Exact river chance has operator norm one on pairwise bounded values. Thus if every ordinary river continuation used in an approximation has a valid uniform pairwise payoff error \(\delta_{b,c}\), then

\[
|V_p(b;i,j)-\widehat V_p(b;i,j)|
\le\sum_cK_c(i,j)\delta_{b,c}
\le\max_c\delta_{b,c}.
\tag{T.13}
\]

For CF values this bound is additionally multiplied by the relevant opponent/chance prefix mass. Arithmetic error and strategic continuation replacement are different quantities; §§7–8 establish when either can enter (T.0). A river local exploitability bound alone is not the \(\delta\) assumed in (T.13).

## 7. Numerical and continuation error: explicit bounds and proofs

This section expands the error leaves R.2.2, T.2.2, F.2.2, and P.2.2. All quantities are in BB unless a normalization is explicitly displayed.

### 7.1 Define the actual candidate being certified

Integer card masks, sorted card labels, action topology, rational sizing, and integer rank codes are exact structural data. Floating-point error cannot justify changing them. In particular, an off-by-one snap or a missing raise is a different EFG, not a small numerical utility perturbation.

For a stored nonnegative legal-action row \(s_a\), define its mathematical behavior as \(s_a/\sum_bs_b\), interpreted exactly; if all entries are zero, define the exact legal uniform row. The auditor encloses this normalized distribution. It thereby certifies the actual stored candidate, even if the training computation used rounded divisions. The same convention can define stored nonnegative boundary factors before the single joint normalization.

If instead a particular external exact range was required and the stored representation approximates it, retain the distribution perturbation margin in §7.5. Do not silently certify a nearby but different prior.

### 7.2 Interval arithmetic down to primitives

For a correctly rounded binary operation, define down/up versions by rounding toward \(-\infty\) or \(+\infty\). If hardware only provides correctly rounded nearest, taking its adjacent representable neighbor in the appropriate direction is a conservative enclosure. The certification kernel must disable unaccounted reassociation, approximate reciprocals, and flush-to-zero behavior, or include their errors.

For intervals \(X=[x^-,x^+]\), \(Y=[y^-,y^+]\), compute:

\[
X+Y=[\operatorname{down}(x^-+y^-),
\operatorname{up}(x^++y^+)],
\]

\[
-X=[-x^+,-x^-],
\]

\[
XY=
[\min_{a\in\{x^-,x^+\},\,b\in\{y^-,y^+\}}\operatorname{down}(ab),
\ \max_{a,b}\operatorname{up}(ab)].
\tag{7.1}
\]

If \(0<y^-\), form \(1/Y=[\operatorname{down}(1/y^+),\operatorname{up}(1/y^-)]\) and multiply. Exact chance denominators are positive integers; their reciprocal intervals follow this rule. Sum reductions apply interval addition at every node in the actual reduction tree. Mask multiplication by exact zero returns exactly zero. For a maximum, use \([\max_a x_a^-,\max_a x_a^+]\).

For input normalization, accumulate an interval for the nonnegative denominator and divide only after its lower endpoint is strictly positive. If an exact positive mass is too small to resolve, increase precision or rescale with tracked exact factors. Do not add an arbitrary epsilon to a denominator. If the true mass is zero, use the defined zero-reach rule instead.

Signed terminal utilities use exact integers or half-integers and interval products with nonnegative prefix weights. Every exact intermediate is enclosed by induction on the arithmetic expression. Applying (7.1) to (R.8), then interval sums/maxima to (R.19), therefore encloses the true legal BR. NaNs indicate failure; infinite upper bounds cannot pass a finite target.

For an alternative analytic roundoff budget, with unit roundoff \(u\) and \(\gamma_m=mu/(1-mu)\), defined only when \(mu<1\), a conventional \(m\)-term floating sum with \((m-1)u<1\) satisfies

\[
|\operatorname{fl}(\textstyle\sum_jx_j)-\sum_jx_j|
\le\gamma_{m-1}\sum_j|x_j|,
\tag{7.2}
\]

under the usual no-overflow/no-unaccounted-underflow assumptions. This follows by expanding each addition's multiplicative error and bounding every product of at most \(m-1\) factors by \(1+\gamma_{m-1}\). A balanced sum replaces the path length by its tree depth \(d\), requiring \(du<1\); a dot product also includes multiplication error. Use the actual reduction graph, not an assumed deterministic order alone. Outward intervals are usually a simpler specification for a final audit.

### 7.3 Counterfactual mass controls errors without division

Define, in own/opponent orientation,

\[
m_p[n,k]=\sum_l\lambda_n^{(p)}[k,l]\rho_{-p}(n,l).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then \(|F_p[n,k]|\le100m_p[n,k]\), and the same holds for \(J_p\). At an opponent node, child masses sum to the parent mass because opponent probabilities sum to one. At chance, they sum to the parent mass because the exact dealer kernel normalizes for each pair. At an own decision, each action child has the same prefix mass as the parent.

**Lemma 1: uniform terminal or continuation errors.** Suppose a replaced terminal payoff or pairwise continuation payoff differs by at most \(\delta\) for every compatible hidden pair. For policy evaluation this need hold for the specified fixed continuation profile; for the BR conclusion it must hold uniformly for **every legal responder continuation** in any replaced suffix. With exact prefix weights, the resulting CF error at any node is at most \(m_p[n,k]\delta\).

**Proof.** At a terminal contraction, the absolute difference is bounded by \(\delta\) times the sum of its nonnegative weights. At an own policy node, a convex combination of child errors is at most \(m_p\delta\). At a BR node, the maximum is 1-Lipschitz in the sup norm and every action has the same mass. Opponent/chance errors add, but so do their masses. Induction proves the claim. Since \(\sum_km_p[e,k]=1\), the root payoff or BR error is at most \(\delta\). The number of sibling boards does not multiply this uniform bound.

At a fixed row and frozen policy, if every \(Q_a\) has error at most \(\eta_a\), then

\[
|\widehat r_a-r_a|
\le\eta_a+\sum_b\sigma_b\eta_b
\le2\max_b\eta_b.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus a uniform \(\delta\) continuation-payoff error yields a regret error at most \(2m_p[n,k]\delta\), before arithmetic error. This is a weighted, unnormalized statement; no division by a tiny counterfactual reach is required.

### 7.4 Expectations, maxima, and perturbed probabilities

For a normalized exact probability row \(p\), an approximate normalized row \(\widehat p\), and values in \([-100,100]\), add and subtract \(\sum_jp_j\widehat V_j\) to obtain

\[
\left|\sum_jp_jV_j-\sum_j\widehat p_j\widehat V_j\right|
\le\sum_jp_j|V_j-\widehat V_j|
+100\|p-\widehat p\|_1.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Approximate values may be clipped to \([-100,100]\) for this argument: clipping cannot increase error against a true value in that interval. Equivalently the kernel term is \(200\,\operatorname{TV}(p,\widehat p)\).

Also,

\[
|\max_ax_a-\max_a\widehat x_a|
\le\max_a|x_a-\widehat x_a|.
\tag{7.6}
\]

To prove (7.6), choose an exact maximizing action and bound its value by the corresponding approximate value plus the maximum error; exchange the two vectors for the reverse inequality.

With an arithmetic envelope \(\eta_n\) and probability-row error \(\xi_n\), a conservative normalized value envelope is

\[
E_n\le\eta_n+100\xi_n+
\begin{cases}
\sum_jp_jE_j,&\text{expectation},\\
\max_jE_j,&\text{maximum}.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.7}
\]

For the hidden-information BR, use its actual CF recurrence and the masses in (7.3), or interval recursion, rather than applying a perfect-information max independently to each hidden pair. A uniform bound valid for every legal behavioral profile remains valid for the optimized BR.

Exact chance has \(\xi_n=0\). Finiteness makes the recursive bound finite. Sequential chance-only runouts propagate errors by expectation, not by the number of complete runouts. If a probability row is not normalized, expectation is not a contraction with coefficient one; fix the candidate's semantics as in §7.1 or explicitly account for its row mass.

### 7.5 Boundary range and normalization error

For two compatible normalized priors \(q,\widehat q\), every fixed legal profile satisfies

\[
|U_p(\sigma;q)-U_p(\sigma;\widehat q)|
\le100\|q-\widehat q\|_1.
\tag{7.8}
\]

The same bound holds uniformly over unilateral deviations because all net utilities remain in \([-100,100]\). Taking maxima preserves it. Therefore for a fixed deployed profile,

\[
|\operatorname{Expl}(\sigma;q)
-\operatorname{Expl}(\sigma;\widehat q)|
\le100\|q-\widehat q\|_1
=200\,\operatorname{TV}(q,\widehat q).
\tag{7.9}
\]

This concerns the **joint compatible prior**, not just its two marginals.

Let \(w,\widehat w\ge0\), \(Z=\sum w>0\), \(\widehat Z=\sum\widehat w>0\), and \(\|w-\widehat w\|_1\le\eta\). Adding and subtracting \(\widehat w/Z\) gives

\[
\left\|\frac wZ-\frac{\widehat w}{\widehat Z}\right\|_1
\le\frac{\eta}{Z}+\frac{|Z-\widehat Z|}{Z}
\le\frac{2\eta}{Z}.
\tag{7.10}
\]

If a verified common lower bound is \(z_{\min}>0\), a safe bound is \(\min(2,2\eta/z_{\min})\). Small boundary mass amplifies normalized error. This is another reason to propagate unnormalized CF vectors through the parent.

For factored ranges under the same exact blocker mask, one sufficient unnormalized bound is

\[
\|M(\beta_0\beta_1^\top-\widehat\beta_0\widehat\beta_1^\top)\|_1
\le
\|\beta_0-\widehat\beta_0\|_1\|\beta_1\|_1
+\|\widehat\beta_0\|_1\|\beta_1-\widehat\beta_1\|_1.
\tag{7.11}
\]

It follows by adding and subtracting \(\widehat\beta_0\beta_1^\top\), applying the triangle inequality, and observing that a Boolean mask can only reduce the entrywise absolute sum.

### 7.6 The sharp BR-to-exploitability error transfer

**Theorem 2.** Fix the deployed profile and the same legal behavioral strategy space. Suppose, for each player,

\[
\sup_{\tau_p}
|U_p(\tau_p,\sigma_{-p})
-\widehat U_p(\tau_p,\sigma_{-p})|\le D_p.
\tag{7.12}
\]

Then

\[
|B_p-\widehat B_p|\le D_p,\qquad
|\operatorname{Expl}-\widehat{\operatorname{Expl}}|
\le\frac{D_0+D_1}{2},
\tag{7.13}
\]

where both games are zero-sum for the exploitability identity.

**Proof.** Pointwise \(U_p\le\widehat U_p+D_p\), so maximizing gives \(B_p\le\widehat B_p+D_p\). Reverse the roles for the other inequality. Add the two BR inequalities and divide by two; the zero-sum on-policy baselines cancel. An individual gain \(g_p=B_p-U_p(\sigma)\) compares two quantities and can need \(2D_p\). Under a common \(D\), the exploitability penalty is \(D\), not \(2D\).

Consequently, if scalar BR estimates satisfy \(B_p\le\widehat B_p+\nu_p\), then

\[
\operatorname{Expl}
\le\frac{\widehat B_0+\widehat B_1+\nu_0+\nu_1}{2}.
\tag{7.14}
\]

Do not separately clamp negative BR payoffs to zero. If the approximating payoffs are not zero-sum, use the actual sum-of-gains definition and include its baseline errors; the cancellation in (7.13) is then unavailable.

### 7.7 Training error, normalization sensitivity, and why certification is separate

The discount map \(D_t(x)=d_t^+x\) for \(x>0\), \(d_t^-x\) for \(x<0\), and zero at zero is continuous and Lipschitz with constant \(d_t^{\max}=\max(d_t^+,d_t^-)\le1\), including across a sign change.

If the instantaneous regret error is bounded by \(\epsilon_t\), and discount arithmetic adds at most \(\eta_t\), then

\[
\|\widehat R^t-R^t\|_\infty
\le d_t^{\max}
\left(\|\widehat R^{t-1}-R^{t-1}\|_\infty+\epsilon_t\right)+\eta_t.
\tag{7.15}
\]

This is proved by applying the Lipschitz bound to the two post-add totals. The premise on \(\epsilon_t\) must include any effect of different policies; one cannot assume the two learning trajectories stay identical merely because arithmetic errors are small.

If two positive-part regret vectors have sums at least \(m>0\), normalized regret matching obeys

\[
\|\operatorname{RM}(R)-\operatorname{RM}(\widehat R)\|_1
\le\frac{2A_n\|R-\widehat R\|_\infty}{m}.
\tag{7.16}
\]

The positive-part map is nonexpansive; normalization costs at most twice the input \(L^1\) error divided by a lower mass bound. Near zero positive regret mass no uniform useful Lipschitz bound exists, and the uniform fallback can differ sharply from a nearly one-hot positive vector.

Thus floating-point learning may follow a different DCFR trajectory. This is compatible with correctness of a final certificate: the final legal candidate is evaluated directly, independently of how it was generated.

The sign-dependent discounted table \(R^T\) is not a sum of regrets under the common averaging weights \(t^2\). Dividing its positive entries by \(\sum_t t^2\) is not a valid exploitability bound. If a regret-based cross-check is wanted, separately accumulate the actual weighted instantaneous regrets

\[
W_p(I,a)=\sum_{t=1}^T t^2r_p^t(I,a).
\]

Define the untruncated weighted external regret in the **same fixed-prior game** on every iteration:

\[
\mathcal R_p^w=\max_{\tau_p}\sum_{t=1}^Tt^2
\left[U_p(\tau_p,\sigma_{-p}^t;q_e)-U_p(\sigma^t;q_e)\right].
\]

Perfect-recall CFR regret decomposition bounds it by
\(\sum_{I\in\mathcal I_p}[\max_aW_p(I,a)]_+\). The induction separates a deviation's first action at each information set from its descendant deviations; their own reach factors lie in \([0,1]\), so dropping them gives the sum of positive local bounds. More explicitly,

\[
\frac{\mathcal R_0^w+\mathcal R_1^w}{\sum_t t^2}
=B_0(\bar\sigma_1;q_e)+B_1(\bar\sigma_0;q_e)
=\operatorname{NashConv}(\bar\sigma;q_e).
\tag{7.17}
\]

Each player's utility is linear in the opponent's realization plan, and the two self-play utility sums cancel, proving the equality. If external regrets are clipped at zero, their sum gives an upper bound instead. This cross-check does not apply across independent solves with changing priors. Computed with certified error it is a valid upper bound, but it does not turn the stored DCFR totals into such a bound. [CFR.]

Equation (7.17) concerns the exact realization-weighted average of the iteration profiles. If stored average accumulators are rounded, a regret-based certificate must also bound the resulting policy discrepancy. The primary BR audit instead directly certifies the normalized stored candidate and needs no assumption that its learning trajectory was numerically exact.

## 8. When local bounds do, and do not, imply a global bound

The following conditions make continuation composition explicit. They are compatible with the imperfect-information decomposition issue studied in [DECOMP], but the bounds used here are derived below rather than delegated to a resolving gadget.

### 8.1 Antichain continuation-error theorem

A frontier \(\mathcal F\) is an antichain of complete public prefixes: no member descends from another. Each cut retains whole information sets. A trajectory enters at most one member. Fix the candidate profile \(\sigma\), and suppose the exact and approximate evaluations have the same legal strategy space, chance, and trunk transitions.

For every legal unilateral deviation \(\tau_p\), let \(d_p^\tau(b)\) be its probability of reaching \(b\) against \(\sigma_{-p}\), and \(q_b^\tau\) its conditional private distribution when that probability is positive. Assume a bound

\[
\left|U_p^b(\tau_p,\sigma_{-p};q_b^\tau)
-\widehat U_p^b(\tau_p,\sigma_{-p};q_b^\tau)\right|
\le\delta_b(q_b^\tau)
\tag{8.1}
\]

for **every such deviation**, including all of its suffix choices. This is a uniform interface condition. It is not a fit to one on-policy scalar value or one fixed-policy pair matrix.

Define

\[
D_p=\sup_{\tau_p}\sum_{b\in\mathcal F}
d_p^\tau(b)\delta_b(q_b^\tau).
\tag{8.2}
\]

**Theorem 3.**

\[
|B_p-\widehat B_p|\le D_p,\qquad
\operatorname{Expl}_{\rm full}
\le\widehat{\operatorname{Expl}}+\frac{D_0+D_1}{2}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Calling \(\widehat{\operatorname{Expl}}\) an exploitability imports Theorem 2's premise of a coherent zero-sum surrogate. For numerical BR evaluation alone, the same upper-bound algebra applies to the half-sum of approximate BR values without asserting the existence of a separate equilibrium game.

**Proof.** Partition terminal histories into those terminating in the trunk and those first entering each \(b\). Trunk terminals have no replacement error. For a fixed deviation, the law of total expectation bounds its root payoff error by the weighted sum in (8.2). Maximize over deviations and apply Theorem 2. If a boundary has zero reach for this deviation, its contribution is zero and no conditional \(q_b^\tau\) needs to be defined.

Because \(\sum_bd_p^\tau(b)\le1\), a common uniform bound \(\delta\) on all siblings gives \(D_p\le\delta\). Summing \(\delta\) once for every board or public history would be unnecessarily loose. Conversely, using only the candidate profile's entry probabilities can be unsound: the maximization is over deviating reaches.

If the approximate interface is used only to certify a BR, (8.1) can bound that evaluation directly. To justify ordinary CFR in an approximate game, it must additionally define a coherent fixed game over the same legal strategies. Calling independent range-dependent equilibrium solvers as scalar leaf functions during updates does not automatically satisfy that premise.

### 8.2 Several streets: telescope incremental errors once

Consider a sequence of hybrid evaluators that replaces river, then turn, then flop interfaces while leaving the candidate strategies and transitions fixed. Let \(\delta_{s,b}\) bound only the **incremental** error of that replacement, excluding an already charged deeper error. For a fixed deviation, telescope the exact-minus-approximate payoff and apply the frontier argument at each street:

\[
D_p\le
\sup_{\tau_p}\sum_s\sum_{b\in\mathcal F_s}
d_p^\tau(b)\delta_{s,b}(q_b^\tau).
\tag{8.4}
\]

This proves the nested bound. At each street the entry probabilities sum to at most one, but a trajectory can enter several streets. If \(\delta_s\) bounds every entry at street \(s\), then \(D_p\le\sum_s\delta_s\).

Do not add both a parent's already propagated total error and the same descendant error again. Either use the recursively computed root envelope or sum only incremental errors. If a replacement changes a deployed strategy, the common-reach payoff telescope is no longer the applicable proof; use §8.4 or recertify the changed full profile.

### 8.3 A composition theorem using local deviation gains

A local exploitability bound is a strategic gap, not automatically a continuation-value error. There is nevertheless a sufficient gain-based composition condition.

For an antichain frontier, let \(G_p^{\rm trunk}\) be the largest gain attainable by changing player \(p\)'s strategy before the frontier while retaining the deployed \(\sigma_p\) afterward. Suppose for **every boundary distribution induced by every such trunk deviation**, the same deployed continuation satisfies

\[
g_p^b(\sigma_b;q_b^\tau)\le\epsilon_{p,b}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Then

\[
g_p^{\rm full}(\sigma)
\le G_p^{\rm trunk}
+\sup_{\tau_p}\sum_{b\in\mathcal F}
d_p^\tau(b)\epsilon_{p,b}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

**Proof.** Split any full deviation into its trunk and suffix behavior. Add and subtract the value of the deviating trunk followed by the original suffix. Its first improvement is bounded by \(G_p^{\rm trunk}\). Conditional on entry at \(b\), the remaining improvement is bounded by (8.5). Weight by entry probabilities, sum, and maximize. Halving the sum of the two player bounds gives exploitability.

For several streets, form hybrids that replace the deviator's behavior one street at a time, using the deployed behavior on later streets. Each incremental improvement is no larger than that street entry's full-suffix one-sided gap under the distribution induced by the preceding deviating streets. This yields the corresponding sum over street frontiers, provided (8.5) holds for all those distributions. It can be loose, but is valid.

The same fixed deployed continuation must satisfy the premise. Finding a different locally solved strategy for each possible range does not prove (8.5) for the assembled profile. A certificate only at its on-policy range is also insufficient.

### 8.4 Pointwise counterfactual security for safe strategy replacement

There is a directly checkable alternative that does not require enumerating all normalized range queries. Replace only the opponent of responder \(p\) inside an antichain of subgames; keep that opponent's strategy outside them fixed.

At frontier \(b\) and responder hand \(k\), retain its original-root prefix weights

\[
w_{p,b}(k,l)=
\text{root/chance probability of }(b,k,l)
\times\text{opponent prefix action reach},
\]

\[
m_{p,b,k}=\sum_lw_{p,b}(k,l).
\tag{8.7}
\]

No responder prefix reach is included. Let \(J_p^{\rm old}(b,k)\) and \(J_p^{\rm new}(b,k)\) be the optimized continuation CF values against the old and new opponent suffix, using these same weights. Both are computable by (R.8) and (R.19).

If for every \(b,k\),

\[
J_p^{\rm new}(b,k)\le J_p^{\rm old}(b,k)+\eta_{p,b,k},
\qquad \eta_{p,b,k}\ge0,
\tag{8.8}
\]

then

\[
B_p^{\rm new}-B_p^{\rm old}
\le
\sup_{\tau_p}\sum_{b,k}
\rho_p^\tau(b,k)\eta_{p,b,k}.
\tag{8.9}
\]

**Proof.** For any responder trunk policy, optimize its suffix separately at each complete boundary observation; this is legal because whole information sets and full-history descendants are retained. The root contribution of \(J_p(b,k)\) is its own prefix factor \(\rho_p^\tau(b,k)\). Substitute (8.8) into the new optimized value for that fixed trunk policy, then maximize over trunk policies. The old maximization is at least the value of that same trunk policy, proving (8.9).

If \(\eta_{p,b,k}\le m_{p,b,k}\delta_b\), (8.9) reduces to a deviation-weighted entry-probability bound because \(\sum_k\rho_p^\tau(b,k)m_{p,b,k}=d_p^\tau(b)\). If all \(\eta=0\), the opponent replacement cannot increase this BR payoff.

For replacing both players, apply the condition separately to each player's opponent replacement and halve the sum of the two margins. For nested replacements, compare each new profile against the current previous profile and telescope the verified security margins.

A rigorous numerical check of (8.8) uses \(J_{\rm new}^{+}\le J_{\rm old}^{-}+\eta\). Valid hands with zero **blueprint own** reach remain in the condition; a responder may give them positive reach. Only zero opponent-and-chance mass makes their contribution necessarily zero. A small local scalar equilibrium gap does not imply these pointwise inequalities.

### 8.5 Explicit failure of frozen-range local certification

The following is a counterexample to an invalid general composition claim, not a numerical assertion about R1 play.

Player 0 privately receives type A or B with probability \(1/2\) each, then chooses Enter or Out. Out pays zero. After Enter, player 1 chooses L or R without knowing the type. Player 0's payoff is:

| Private type | L | R |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A | 0 | 0 |
| B | \(+M\) | \(-M\) |

Consider the profile “A enters, B exits, player 1 chooses L.” The entering range contains only A. Its continuation has exact exploitability zero and equilibrium value zero. Globally, B can deviate to Enter, so \(B_0=M/2\), \(B_1=0\), and global exploitability is \(M/4\).

The issue persists with positive support. Let B enter with probability \(0<\zeta\le1\). Its conditional entry mass is \(\zeta/(1+\zeta)\), so local exploitability is \(M\zeta/(1+\zeta)\), tending to zero. Yet \(B_0=M/2\), \(B_1=M\zeta/2\), and global exploitability is \(M(1+\zeta)/4\), tending to \(M/4\). A rare type can become frequent under a profitable earlier deviation. Full support at one reference range does not create a uniform guarantee.

### 8.6 What the requested pot-relative tolerances can certify

For chosen street interfaces define a deviation-weighted pot envelope

\[
\mathcal A_p=
\sup_{\tau_p}\sum_s\sum_{b\in\mathcal F_s}
d_p^\tau(b)P_b.
\tag{8.10}
\]

If \(\delta_{s,b}=0.005P_b\) is a valid **uniform incremental payoff approximation error** satisfying §§8.1–8.2, then a sufficient bound is

\[
\operatorname{Expl}_{\rm full}
\le\widehat{\operatorname{Expl}}
+0.0025(\mathcal A_0+\mathcal A_1)+\nu,
\tag{8.11}
\]

where \(\nu\) covers numerical certification error and is not already included elsewhere.

If instead the available quantities are **local exploitability** bounds \(0.005P_b\), then even under the stronger uniform-range premise of §8.3 they imply only \(\epsilon_{p,b}\le0.01P_b\). Here \(G_p^{\rm trunk}\) allows behavior changes before the **first** selected street frontier, while \(\mathcal A_p\) covers the selected downstream street entries, charging each newly changed street once. With \(E_{\rm trunk}=(G_0^{\rm trunk}+G_1^{\rm trunk})/2\), the resulting sufficient bound is

\[
\operatorname{Expl}_{\rm full}
\le E_{\rm trunk}
+0.005(\mathcal A_0+\mathcal A_1)+\nu.
\tag{8.12}
\]

Use measured one-sided gaps or pointwise security margins for tighter budgets. The quantities in (8.11) and (8.12) have different hypotheses and are not interchangeable.

To establish the original preflop target, the chosen upper bound must be at most **0.0075 BB**. Later entry pots can greatly exceed 1.5 BB: the actual \(C=28\) flop entries have \(P_e=56\) BB and local tolerance 0.28 BB. Neither multiplying every later pot by 0.005 nor adding conditional local certificates proves the root requirement. Deviating occupancies, error type, range coverage, and remaining numerical margin all matter.

The exact full-game BR test (P.0) avoids these decomposition assumptions entirely. It certifies the assembled profile directly, regardless of the training schedule used to obtain it.

## 9. Complete execution specification and implementation boundaries

### 9.1 Reference loop with no unspecified evaluator or optimizer

The following pseudocode is an execution schedule for the equations already derived. “Compile” expands the source grammar and table in §§2–6. “Contract” means the masked products and reductions in (R.8). “BR” means (R.19), with the interval primitives in §7.2.

~~~text
INPUT:
    exact entry public history e, exact compatible prior q_e
    entry pot P_e (1.5 BB at original preflop root)
    exact rational target epsilon = P_e / 200

COMPILE:
    create a unique public node for every generated transcript
    attach exact actor/mode/board/contributions/targets
    attach canonical chance-event indices and compatibility masks
    retain every legal physical private-hand information-set row
    obtain forward and reverse topological orders

INITIALIZE:
    R = 0, S = 0 on all legal action rows
    sigma = uniform on each legal deduplicated action set
    invalid hand rows and padding = 0
    t = 1

REPEAT:
    1. Freeze sigma^t = masked regret matching of R^(t-1).       [R.3]
    2. Forward:
         rho_p(e,k) = 1 for compatible entry hands
         kappa(e) = 1
         player edge: update only its actor's rho by sigma^t  [R.6]
         chance edge: copy rho; apply child card support;
                      multiply kappa by exact reciprocal denominator
    3. For each traverser p:
         terminal: contract hidden hands with q_e, kappa,
                   blockers, opponent rho, and exact net utility
         reverse nodes:
             own actor: Q[a] = F[child_a]
                        F = sum_a sigma^t[a] * Q[a]
                        r[a] = Q[a] - F
             opponent or chance: F = sum_child F[child]
    4. Accumulate every legal row's old-profile own-reach strategy:
         S = (t/(t+1))^2 * (S + rho_actor^t * sigma^t)          [R.2]
    5. Commit regrets simultaneously:
         X = R + r
         R = where(X>0, t^(3/2)/(t^(3/2)+1)*X,
                       where(X<0, X/2, 0))                  [R.4]
    6. At a requested checkpoint:
         average = normalize S by legal action sum;
                   zero sum => exact legal uniform fallback
         fix this stored candidate and its exact normalization semantics
         for p in {SB, BB}:
             recompute only the fixed opponent's action reaches
             contract terminal intervals
             reverse: own node => max of legal child intervals
                                  (illegal padded lanes use -infinity);
                      opponent/chance => sum of child intervals
             B_p_interval = sum over entry own-hand intervals
         E_upper = outward_upper((B_SB_upper + B_BB_upper)/2)
         if E_upper <= exact epsilon (or its downward-rounded value):
             return candidate and full certificate
    7. t = t + 1
~~~

A resource or iteration limit may stop a run, but its output is then an **uncertified candidate** unless the check has passed. This derivation asserts no practical iteration count, measured exploitability, or successful full-game solve.

### 9.2 Zero-reach rules collected in one place

| Situation | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Hand intersects board or other pair intersects it | Exact support mask; no contribution |
| Padded action | Probability, regret, and average entry zero |
| Valid row, no positive regrets | Uniform over legal finalized actions |
| Own prefix action reach zero, CF mass positive | Still compute/update regrets and BR; add zero to that round's average row |
| Opponent-and-chance CF mass zero | CF values and instantaneous regrets zero; retain row and apply scheduled discounts |
| Average row sum zero | Legal uniform exported policy |
| Boundary joint normalizer zero | Conditional local solve/certificate undefined; global CF history retained |
| Valid hand has zero fixed root prior | No weight in this fixed-prior solve; retain a specified legal policy for other queries |
| Zero blueprint reach but positive deviation reach | Include in full-game BR and security conditions |

Do not threshold small positive reach to zero without accounting for the omitted mass uniformly under deviations. Applying a scheduled discount lazily is permitted only if the exact product of missed discounts and their sign behavior is respected.

### 9.3 GPU scheduling and safe reuse

The reference requires only integer mask/compare operations; gather/scatter; rank and action reductions; prefix scans; real multiplication, addition, division, and square root for the scalar discount; and topologically ordered tensor contractions.

Batch different complete public histories with the same tensor dimensions for throughput. Keep their row IDs distinct. Stream opponent-hand tiles for terminal contractions, use reverse-frontier buffers for \(F,J\), and offload persistent regret/average tables when necessary. A tile boundary must not cause a second normalization of an opponent range or omit a partial sum.

For factored fixed entry ranges, one optional exact scaling is

\[
F_p[n,k]=\frac{\kappa_n\beta_p[k]}{Z_e}G_p[n,k].
\tag{9.1}
\]

Where \(\beta_p[k]>0\), this factor is positive and independent of iteration at that information set. DCFR and regret matching are positively homogeneous, so using correspondingly scaled \(G\)-regrets gives identical strategies. Its terminal is
\(G_p[z,k]=\sum_lM_z^{(p)}[k,l]\beta_{-p}[l]\rho_{-p}(z,l)u_p(z;k,l)\).
Own and opponent decision backups are as before, but chance now uses
\(G_p[n,k]=(1/d)\sum_cG_p[n_c,k]\).
This is a distinct normalization convention; **do not combine its backward chance factors with the forward-weighted \(F\) convention**. At zero \(\beta_p[k]\), no division/equivalence is claimed; retain an explicit legal extension.

Normalization by the current opponent mass is generally iteration dependent and would change the regret sequence. Likewise, independently normalizing boundary factors requires retaining their scale factors in parent transfers.

The storage issue is substantial even before betting branching. One legal limp/check, flop-check-through, turn-check-through path reaches
\(\binom{52}{3}\cdot49\cdot48=51{,}979{,}200\)
distinct labelled river boards. Its BB river entry alone has
\(51{,}979{,}200\cdot1081=56{,}189{,}515{,}200\)
physical-hand information sets. Dense six-action FP64 regret and average tables for just that slice require about \(5.39\times10^{12}\) bytes. This arithmetic is not a count of the whole game; it illustrates why full-history tabulation needs substantial storage or an exact recomputation/distribution plan. It does not justify merging rows.

Structural validation should include the strict half-up/minimum/snap boundary cases; both \(C=28\) prefixes; root limp and BB option; minimum raises and deduplication; short all-ins; called-all-in chance scheduling; and fold accounting before any future cards.
